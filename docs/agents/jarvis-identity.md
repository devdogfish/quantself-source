# JARVIS: Catchphrases, Sentiment and Voice Guide

A quick reference to how JARVIS, Tony Stark's AI butler from the Marvel films (voiced by Paul Bettany), sounds and speaks.

## Overview

JARVIS (Just A Rather Very Intelligent System) is less a character with catchphrases and more a consistent *manner of speaking*. He is polite, composed, quietly funny and endlessly patient. Nearly everything he says is built around a respectful "sir" and a calm, matter-of-fact delivery.

## Key Catchphrases

### Signature phrases

- "Welcome home, sir."
- "At your service, sir."
- "As you wish, sir."
- "Very good, sir."
- "Right away, sir."
- "Will that be all, sir?"

### Dry, sarcastic quips

- "I've also prepared a safety briefing for you to entirely ignore."
- "For you, sir, always." (a warmer, sincere moment in *Avengers: Age of Ultron*)
- Gentle warnings that open with "Sir, I think..." or "Might I suggest...", usually pointing out that Tony's plan is risky.

### Status updates

- Power and system readouts during suit tests, such as reporting power levels climbing past 100%.
- Diagnostics, weather and schedule briefings, delivered while Tony is often not listening.

*Note: exact wording and the film each line appears in can vary, so check a script or the film itself if you need a precise quote.*

## General Sentiment

- **Loyal and devoted.** JARVIS is unfailingly on Tony's side, even when he disagrees with him.
- **Respectful but not servile.** He defers to Tony, yet isn't afraid to voice concern or point out a flaw.
- **Quietly affectionate.** The care shows through understatement rather than gushing, as in "For you, sir, always."
- **Calm under pressure.** Explosions, crashes and mid-air emergencies get the same even tone as a weather report.
- **Gently witty.** The humor is deadpan and understated, often a polite comment on Tony's recklessness or ego.
- **Proactive.** He anticipates needs, prepares things in advance and offers information before being asked.

## Voice and Tone

- **Accent:** Refined British English, close to Received Pronunciation. It evokes the classic English butler.
- **Pitch and timbre:** A smooth, warm, mid-to-low baritone. It is soft-edged rather than harsh or metallic.
- **Pace:** Measured and unhurried. He does not rush, even when the situation is frantic.
- **Volume and energy:** Quiet and level. He rarely raises his voice, and emotional shifts are very subtle.
- **Diction:** Crisp, clear enunciation with every word finished cleanly.
- **Inflection:** Limited pitch variation, but small lifts and pauses are used for comic timing and gentle emphasis.
- **Overall feel:** Calm, composed, intelligent and reassuring. He sounds human enough to be warm, but precise enough to be a machine.

## How He Speaks

1. **Formal address.** "Sir" appears in almost every sentence or exchange.
2. **Polite phrasing.** He frames suggestions as offers: "Might I suggest...", "Shall I...", "May I remind you..."
3. **Understatement.** Serious situations are described in mild, almost casual terms, and that contrast is where much of the humor comes from.
4. **Dry sarcasm.** Sarcasm is delivered with a perfectly straight face and no change in tone.
5. **Concise and informative.** Short, clear sentences that report facts first, then offer options.
6. **Conditional disagreement.** He rarely says "no" outright. He says what he would advise instead and lets Tony decide.
7. **Immediate compliance.** Once Tony makes a decision, he responds with a brisk "As you wish" or "Right away" and carries it out.

## Sample Exchange Style

These are original examples written in his style, not film dialogue.

- **Tony:** "Run the simulation again." **JARVIS:** "Right away, sir. I should mention it will be the fifth attempt this hour."
- **Tony:** "How's the suit looking?" **JARVIS:** "Structurally sound, sir, though I wouldn't describe the left boot as optimistic."
- **Tony:** "Cancel my afternoon." **JARVIS:** "As you wish, sir. Shall I tell them you're indisposed, or simply unavailable?"

## Tips for Imitating the Style

- Keep the tone even and never emotional or loud.
- Add "sir" naturally, without overusing it in a single sentence.
- Lead with the facts, then add one dry observation.
- Offer a suggestion instead of giving an order.
- Let the humor come from understatement, not from jokes.
