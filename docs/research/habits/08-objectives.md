# What are we optimizing for? (Atomic Habits)

Sourced from the audiobook transcript. Short quotes only.

## Objectives, in Clear's order of importance

1. **Become the person.** Clear says habits matter less for results than because they change your beliefs about yourself (ch. 2). Every action is "a vote for the type of person you wish to become". The process is to decide who you want to be, then prove it with small wins. *This is the goal; everything else is the means.*
2. **Keep showing up (systems over goals).** "Forget about goals. Focus on systems instead." (ch. 1) Stick with it long enough to get through the plateau where effort shows no visible result yet. Missing once is an accident; never miss twice (ch. 16).
3. **Make it automatic, to free attention.** Habits form through frequency, not time (ch. 11). Automatic habits "do not restrict freedom. They create it." They make the fundamentals easy, so energy goes to what matters (ch. 3, 19).
4. **Then grow it, without getting complacent.** "Habits plus deliberate practice equal mastery." Once one habit runs on autopilot, return to effort with the next one (ch. 20).
5. **Reflect and review.** Annual review: what went well, what didn't, what did I learn. Integrity report: am I living by my values, and how can I raise the standard? (ch. 20)

## Guardrail

Goodhart's law (ch. 16): "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure." Measurement should guide, not consume. So no aggregate score to chase. Numbers serve the identity question.
